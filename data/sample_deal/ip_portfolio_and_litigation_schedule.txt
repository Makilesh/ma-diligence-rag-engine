SECTION 2: THE DATAFLOW SYSTEMS LITIGATION
================================================================================

Caption:        DataFlow Systems Inc. v. Aurora Technologies Inc.
Case Number:    2023-CV-04821
Court:          U.S. District Court, Northern District of California
Filed:          September 14, 2023
Claim:          Patent infringement

DataFlow Systems Inc. alleges that Aurora's real-time data reconciliation engine
infringes U.S. Patent Nos. 9,847,221 and 10,112,905, both directed to methods
for incremental reconciliation of distributed record sets.

The four Aurora patents most closely related to the accused functionality are
U.S. Patent Nos. 10,445,882, 10,662,013, 11,004,776 and 11,238,450 — all naming
Dr. James Wu as first inventor.

Procedural posture as of March 15, 2024:
  - Aurora's motion to dismiss was denied in part on January 8, 2024.
  - Claim construction (Markman) hearing is scheduled for August 2024.
  - No trial date has been set. Counsel estimates trial no earlier than Q3 2025.

Exposure assessment by outside counsel (Wilson Sonsini):
  - Probable exposure range: $3.5 million to $8.0 million.
  - Counsel assesses the probability of an injunction as low, on the basis that
    DataFlow does not practise the asserted patents and would face difficulty
    demonstrating irreparable harm.
  - A licensing settlement in the range of $4 million to $6 million is
    considered the most likely outcome.

Financial statement treatment: the Company has NOT recorded a loss accrual, on
the basis that no loss is both probable and reasonably estimable within the
meaning of ASC 450. The range is disclosed but not accrued. Buyer's accounting
advisers have flagged this as an area of judgement.

Legal fees of $2.8 million were incurred in FY2023 in connection with this
matter and are treated as a non-recurring add-back in the quality of earnings
report. That add-back is qualified: the litigation is unresolved and comparable
fees are likely in FY2024.

SECTION 4: OPEN SOURCE SOFTWARE
================================================================================

The Company's software incorporates open source components. A software
composition analysis was performed by Black Duck in January 2024.

  Components identified                                     1,847
  Permissive licences (MIT, Apache 2.0, BSD)                1,792
  Weak copyleft (LGPL, MPL)                                    51
  Strong copyleft (GPL, AGPL)                                   4

The four strong copyleft components are:

  1. A GPL-licensed database driver used only in an internal build tool. Not
     distributed to customers. No copyleft obligation is triggered.

  2. An AGPL-licensed graph rendering library linked into the customer-facing
     analytics module. THIS IS A MATERIAL FINDING. AGPL obligations may extend
     to network-delivered software. The Company has engaged counsel and is
     replacing the component. Estimated remediation cost: $340,000, targeted
     for completion in Q3 2024.

  3. and 4. Two GPL command-line utilities invoked as separate processes. Counsel
     advises that process separation avoids derivative work treatment.

The merger agreement representation at Section 3.9(f) regarding open source
compliance is qualified by disclosure of the AGPL component described above.
